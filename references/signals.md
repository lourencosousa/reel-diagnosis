# What the numbers mean

## What Instagram ranks on

Adam Mosseri, head of Instagram, has named three signals as the ones that matter most (January 2025, repeated since):

- **Watch time.** Does the reel hold people?
- **Likes per reach.** Does it land with the people who saw it? Weighs most with followers.
- **Sends per reach.** Do people send it to a friend? Weighs most for reaching people who do not follow the account. Instagram calls these shares.

Everything else is secondary. Mosseri has said Instagram weighs sends far more than comments, and that sends per reach matter most for reaching new people. Saves say the reel is useful, not that it spreads.

## Each number is a question

| Number | The question it answers | Where it comes from in the video |
|---|---|---|
| Skip rate (left in the first 3 seconds) | Did the opening stop the scroll? | The first frame, the first line, the first cut |
| Average watch time, and the retention graph | Did the middle keep its promise? | Every drop in the graph has a cause at that second |
| Views by source: Reels tab and Explore | Did Instagram trust it with strangers? | Watch time and sends from the first test audience |
| Followers versus non-followers | Who it reached | High non-followers: it travelled. Low: it stayed home |
| Likes per view | Did the people who watched it like it? | The payoff, and how much they care about the person |
| Shares per view | Is it worth sending to someone? | A line or moment that says something about the sender or the receiver |
| Saves per view | Is it worth coming back to? | Information, a place, a recipe, a list |
| Comments, and what they say | Did it provoke? What exactly? | The words in the comments name the moment that worked |
| Follows | Did it make people want more of this person? | A clear point of view, a series, a face |

Use per view rates (likes divided by views), not totals. On someone else's reel, plays stand in for reach.

**Sends you can see.** Shares are private on someone else's reel, but a comment that tags a friend ("@anna this is you") is a send made in public. Count the tagging comments among the ones you can read. Several tags with no other text is the strongest public sign that a reel is sendable. Only emojis means people liked it but had nothing to pass on.

## Patterns

Place the reel on two axes against the account's own reels: reach (plays versus the median) and resonance (likes per play versus the median).

| | Resonance high | Resonance low |
|---|---|---|
| **Reach high** | **Breakout.** Find the lever and repeat it. | **Reached, did not land.** The opening or paid push earned views, the content did not earn reactions. Suspect a boost, a hook that oversold, or a payoff that never came. |
| **Reach low** | **Loved, did not travel.** Followers liked it, strangers never saw it. Suspect the opening (skip rate) or nothing worth sending. | **Missed.** Look at the opening first: if people left in 3 seconds, nothing after it mattered. |

"High" and "low" mean clearly above or below the account's median, roughly 1.5 times either way. Close to the median is "in line", and the diagnosis then looks for what kept it from going further.

## Things that bend the numbers

- **Paid partnership and branded reels** can be boosted with ad money. Plays then rise with no matching rise in likes or comments. On your own reel, Insights splits organic from ad views.
- **Trending audio** can lift reach on its own. Note when the reel uses it.
- **Age.** Reels keep growing for days. A reel younger than 3 days is not finished: say so in the verdict.
- **Hidden likes** show as nothing. Use comments as the resonance measure instead, and say so.
- **Collaborations** show the same numbers on both accounts. Compare with the account that posted it.
- **Recycled or watermarked content** is pushed less by Instagram on purpose.
